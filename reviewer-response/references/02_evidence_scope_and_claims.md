# Evidence, Versions, and Historical Matching

## Identify the Sources

| Source role | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| Review-round baseline | The manuscript actually reviewed in this round, often called `raw`. Keep it unchanged within the round. |
| Current working manuscript | The ongoing revised manuscript and supplement. Use their latest contents for current quotations and proposed-edit anchors. |
| Prior responses and revision records | Sources of earlier actions, explanations, evidence, wording, and decisions; recheck anything reused. |
| Verified outputs and current figures/tables | Evidence of actual results and displayed material; manuscript descriptions alone do not prove execution. |
| Proposed next-version text | Wording not yet applied to the current working files. |

At a new review round, identify the version actually sent for that round rather than continuing to compare everything with the earliest manuscript. Do not equate a filename or modification time with scientific authority.

Apply current author instructions and project contracts. Distinguish verified current evidence, author-confirmed specifications, historical records, inference, and unresolved information. Investigate contradictions between manuscript text and outputs instead of silently choosing a convenient source.

## Match a Request to Revision History

1. Locate the relevant current passages, figures, and tables. Then search prior replies and revision records for the same requested action, object, and qualifiers.
2. Check substantive equivalence, not keyword overlap alone. Related metrics, analysis units, models, or validation requests may answer different questions.
3. Compare the matching passages with the review-round baseline and the current working version. Check any reused numerical claim against its supporting output.
4. Classify the match using the table below and retain only the material that answers this comment. Describe the result briefly in the response strategy; do not impose a separate tracking form.
5. Identify remaining gaps. A historical claim of completion is insufficient when the current files or verified outputs do not support it.

| Match | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Already in the baseline and still present | Use the evidence without calling it newly added. |
| Revised in this round and present now | Describe the verified revision and quote current text where needed. |
| Partial match | Reuse the relevant portion and separately answer the remaining request. |
| Historical record unsupported or superseded now | Reconcile its status before reusing the wording or completion claim. |
| No verified match | State the gap and identify the necessary next action without inventing an analysis. |

## Evidence and Scope

Select the smallest evidence set that answers the operative request. Reviewer examples do not automatically require new analyses or limitations. Existing analyses may answer the reviewer even when unchanged from the baseline.

Use a suggested example when it clarifies the answer; otherwise omit it without explaining why it was unnecessary. Do not announce that an analysis was not performed when the reviewer did not request that analysis. Keep the baseline-to-current comparison in preparation notes and translate it into an accurate response action, such as clarifying an existing rule, rather than recounting internal version history.

Use response-only evidence directly when sufficient. Do not add manuscript text merely to mirror it, and do not add a defensive announcement that it was not incorporated. Propose new analysis only for a demonstrated unanswered request and within the author's execution authorization.

Preserve observation units, dependencies, grouping, compared quantities, uncertainty, and adjustment status when reporting supplied results. Do not relabel repeated observations as independent replication or classify an analysis solely by whether it uses patients, samples, regions, or cells.

For multiple-testing results, distinguish nominal and adjusted evidence and retain the available effect estimates and uncertainty. Compare the same named quantity, model, or gene set before comparing numerical results; flag unavailable required information rather than calculating or inventing it during drafting.

Keep association, prediction, and causation distinct. A retrospective association, computational inference, or subgroup observation is not automatically a validated mechanism or clinical recommendation. State central unmet validation needs directly while accurately presenting the completed evidence.
