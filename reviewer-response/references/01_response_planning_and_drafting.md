# Response Planning and Drafting

Use one numbered reviewer comment as the response unit. In a complete letter, reproduce the full verbatim reviewer comment immediately before its `Response:`, retaining the original numbering and major/minor hierarchy; a numbered heading alone is not the comment. Omit reproduced comments only when the author explicitly requests response text alone. Keep planning discussion in the requested delivery channel.

## Opening and Acknowledgement

Start with bold `Response:` followed on the same line by one brief, concern-specific sentence. Do not bold the whole opening paragraph. Name the actual request or concern and preserve its important qualifiers.

| Opening | Use |
|---|---|
| `We thank the reviewer for ...` | Thank the reviewer for a specific observation, question, or suggestion. |
| `We appreciate the reviewer's concern/request/recommendation ...` | Recognize the particular concern or requested action. |
| `We agree that ...` | State agreement with a proposition the authors actually accept. |
| `We apologize that ...` | Acknowledge an established error, omission, or unclear description that warrants an apology. |
| `We acknowledge that ...` | Recognize a verified fact, limitation, or shortcoming; do not concede an unverified premise. |
| `We acknowledge the reviewer's concern about ...` | Recognize the concern without implying agreement with every premise; answer it substantively afterward. |

`We acknowledge this limitation` may follow a clear statement of the limitation. Acknowledgement can open the response or follow a thank-you sentence when it adds substance. It is neither an automatic apology nor a substitute for answering the request.

Vary openings across adjacent comments. Avoid generic praise, routine intensifiers such as `fully`, and claims that the revisions have substantially improved the paper without explaining the actual change.

## Action Words and Completion Status

- Use `revised` for a general text change; use `expanded` only when substantive detail or scope increased.
- Reserve `added` and `performed` for genuinely new content or work. Use `clarified` or `revised the presentation` for an existing analysis made easier to understand or find.
- Do not call an unmet request `addressed`. Distinguish completed work from a proposed change or unresolved need.
- Keep source-reconciliation notes and drafting-status explanations in author-facing preparation notes. State an unfinished request in the response when it answers the reviewer's concern; identify unapplied wording in its proposed-change material rather than narrating internal version bookkeeping throughout the response.
- Preserve an explicitly specified author-approved tense or release-status formulation; do not add unsolicited conditional wording or workflow reminders. If the requested statement conflicts with verified evidence, resolve the conflict before presenting it as fact.

## Body Assembly

1. For several distinct response elements, add one brief bridge explaining how the concern is answered and how the points are organized. Use `analyses` only for analyses actually performed.
2. Use short descriptive bullet lead-ins, such as `Input dimensions.` or `Training strategy.` Bold the lead-in, not its entire paragraph. Let the reviewer's requests determine the grouping.
3. Use continuous prose for a single coherent point. Sequential `First` and `Second` paragraphs are also suitable; do not repeat their contents in a parallel bullet overview.
4. State the action, relevant result, interpretation, and evidence. Explanations, corrections, acknowledged limitations, and evidence-based disagreement are all permitted when they answer the comment.
5. When inserting a figure for an analysis, state the analytical action before the insertion. Keep pre-insertion bullets limited to actions or evaluated elements, without their outcomes; report the associated results and interpretation after the labeled item, once. Manual-insertion directions belong in the placeholder, not the substantive explanation. Follow the item-label rules in `03_manuscript_changes_and_matrices.md`.
6. Finish the substantive answer before manuscript quotations. Put this comment's modification block at its end rather than collecting modifications from several comments into one shared block.
7. Keep overview replies concise. Do not repeat evidence fully presented in adjacent numbered replies merely for continuity. Preserve all author-approved evidence when compressing an answer.

Use one substantive account of each evidence item. If a table supplies the complete numerical comparison, use prose for its conclusion and necessary interpretation, not a second numerical inventory. When the result directly answers the request, omit an additional sentence that merely repeats it as an interpretation. Do not append a repetitive `Together` summary after an already complete overview.

## Draft Equations

Write mathematical expressions as LaTeX enclosed by single dollar signs. Do not use parenthesized delimiters, double-dollar display delimiters, fenced formula blocks, or plain-text pseudo-formulas. Ordinary numbers do not automatically become equations. Word serialization is governed by `04_document_editing_and_validation.md`.
